Further primary-source review, 12 November 2026

1. Scout Before You Route (Milios and Pal, submitted 6 Nov 2026)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09028
Predicts success and output length separately using a frozen small LLM's
prefill activations. The paper reports stronger usefulness of query-dependent
costs for reasoning models. Its predictor requires a neural scout/hidden states,
which our contest API and prohibition on local neural models do not permit.
Decision: retain separate per-model cost/outcome labels and CPU-only features;
do not import its network or claim its savings for our harness.

2. On the Fundamental Limits of Prefill-Based Decoding Length Prediction
(Shi et al., submitted 29 Oct 2026)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40541
Studies stochastic variation in decoding length, and argues for fixed target
model/data/sampling conditions plus relative and absolute errors. Its theory
does not establish a numerical error floor for our multi-call agent policies.
Decision: keep four distinct model scopes, compare predictors on the same
measured trajectories, report MAE and WAPE, and avoid exact-cost guarantees.

3. Agentic Abstention (Luo et al., NeurIPS 2026 project)
https://agentic-abstention.github.io/
Distinguishes request-visible impossibility from blockers revealed by tools,
and measures the delay before a justified abstention. CONVOLVE transfers a
playbook of lessons from trajectories. Its displayed heldout intervention is
on abstention tasks and does not establish preserved solvable-task accuracy.
Decision: evaluate cost and extra calls on negatives together with positive
task reward and false hard skips. A classifier saying UNSOLVABLE is not a
proof. Keep concrete tool feedback/current candidate for repair decisions.

All three are research evidence motivating experiments, not independently
verified performance claims about our implementation. No third-party code or
weights from these sources were copied into the production container.

Additional CPU-only research review, 13 November 2026

4. Bayesian Optimization With Censored Response Data
(Hutter, Hoos, Leyton-Brown, 2013; expanded workshop paper)
https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1947
Uses classical random forests with EM-style sampled imputation from truncated
predictive distributions to retain lower-bound information from stopped runs.
Sampling rather than imputing one mean helps retain uncertainty. Its evidence
concerns algorithm configuration, not Qwen agent token forecasts.
Our inference: retain stop_cause and trustworthy spent-cost lower bounds for
future censor-aware fitting. An external interruption of the same fixed policy
can censor its completion cost. A normal stop prescribed by that policy is an
observed complete policy cost; finish_reason=length alone is not censoring.
With very few censored training observations, do not add a survival model now.
No code copied and no new dependency proposed.

5. Restart Economics for AI Agents: Cutoffs, Retries, and Cost per Resolved Task
(James E. Murphy, working paper v0.1, 28 September 2026; not peer-reviewed evidence)
https://jamesemurphy.com/ai-economics/restart-economics/
https://jamesemurphy.com/ai-economics/restart-economics/restart-economics-ai-agents.pdf
The primary paper evaluates logged fresh OpenHands attempts with train/heldout
cross-fitting. A low-complexity state rule combines logistic success prediction
and quasi-Poisson remaining-cost regression, eight coefficients per configuration.
It finds little extra value from this rule beyond retries, and documents that its
commit-to-termination approximation can stop too early. Monetary engineer-fallback
costs and fresh restarts differ from our lexicographic contest objective and repair
continuations. Cross-model replay adds an independence assumption not established
by the logs. These numerical savings must not be transferred to our harness.
Our possible adaptation: after an actual call, estimate marginal success gain and
additional cost from visible failure/verification feedback, and compare the use
of the remaining budget with attempting another task. This needs observed training
continuations and trustworthy outcome labels; unexecuted retries are not known
counterfactual outcomes. Public test passing is not hidden correctness.
The reproducibility section states MIT for code; the source trajectories have no
declared license and are not redistributed by that repository. No code/data copied.

Decision: first independently evaluate the already-frozen residual predictor;
only then consider a small predeclared retry-policy experiment. These sources
motivate tests and failure checks, not a claim that an additional model will help.
